DJ with the Deluge

Manual for the DJ functions of the Deluge firmware 1.2.1-mastertune v19.0 and newer: Scan, Sync, Nudge and the DJ filter

With v19.0 the Deluge plays along with a track from an external player. It hears the track's tempo and key (Scan), takes over the tempo with one press (Sync), lets you push its beat onto the track's by ear, like a jog wheel (Nudge), and gives the whole song a DJ mixer's filter on one knob (DJ filter). This manual shows how to wire it, how each function works and how they fit together in a mix.

The big picture. Beatmatching has two parts: the same tempo, and the beats at the same moment (the phase). Sync does the first with one press. The second is done by ear: start the Deluge on the track's first beat of a bar, then nudge until the kick drums land together. The DJ filter then gives you the classic moves for transitions and build-ups. The track comes from any player with a line-level output: a phone, a laptop, a CDJ or a media player.

2. What you need

  • Firmware v19.0 or newer (latest: v19.0.4, deluge-1.2.1-mastertune-v19.0.4-l2d-92b7ebbe.bin). Settings → Firmware version shows V19.0.4-L2D. SHIFT + press SELECT opens Settings.
  • A player with a line-level output and a stereo cable to the Deluge's LINE IN. The Deluge has no input gain: you set the level at the player.
  • Headphones or speakers on the Deluge. The headphones carry the same mix as the main outputs. The Deluge mixes in stereo only with headphones or the right line output plugged in; with only the internal speaker or the left output everything is mono.
  • Optional: a DJ mixer (setup B, chapter 3.2), and gear that follows the Deluge's MIDI or trigger clock: it follows sync and nudge.

3. Setting up

3.1 Setup A: the player through the Deluge

The player comes into the Deluge and out with your song (figure 1). You hear everything from the Deluge, and the DJ filter works on the player as well.

  1. Cable: the player's output into the Deluge's LINE IN.
  2. An audio track for the input: in Song view press a pad in an empty row and keep holding it (a new clip appears), then press SELECT: the clip becomes an audio clip on its own audio track. (This works only while the new clip is still empty.)
  3. Its input: hold LEARN and press the audio clip's pad: the menu AUDIO SOURCE opens. Turn SELECT to STEREO INPUT (MONITORING) (7-segment: STER., with the dot) and leave with BACK. Only the options with (monitoring) pass the input through. The same menu is in the audio clip's own menu (enter the clip, press SELECT, Audio source).
  4. Test: start the player. You hear it from the Deluge at once, also while the song is stopped. Set the level at the player (chapter 3.3); the audio track's volume also works on it.

The monitored input passes the audio track's own effects, then the song's effects and the DJ filter. Save the song: the track and its input are kept, so next time only the cable and the player are needed.

3.2 Setup B: with a DJ mixer

The Deluge is one deck of the mixer, the player another. The Deluge only listens to the player to read its tempo, so no monitoring track: you would hear the player twice. The mixer's cue lets you pre-listen to the Deluge on headphones before you bring it in, which the Deluge alone cannot do. Here the DJ filter works on the Deluge's channel only.

For the split, a Y cable at the player's output works (a line output can feed two inputs), or a second output of the player or of the mixer (booth, record or send out).

3.3 The input level

Open the Scan view (chapter 5): its sidebar (the audition column, the rightmost) shows the input's level, from −60 dB at the bottom pad to 0 dB at the top, 7.5 dB a pad. Set the player so that the loud parts light 5 to 7 green pads and the top pad, red, stays dark (it lights above about −4 dB). Too quiet (fewer than 3 pads) makes the tempo hard to read; into the red, the input clips.

4. Quick start

  1. Set up as in chapter 3.1. Start the track on the player.
  2. In Song view: SHIFT + press SELECT → Tuning → Scan: note, BPM, key. The OLED shows SCAN LINE.
  3. Press SELECT until the BPM is big. After about 4 to 5 s of music it shows the tempo, e.g. 128.0 BPM.
  4. If it shows half or double of what you hear, turn ◀▶: BPM X2 or BPM /2.
  5. Press TAP TEMPO: SYNC 128.0 BPM. The song now has the track's tempo.
  6. BACK to Song view. Count along with the track and press PLAY on a one (the first beat of a bar).
  7. Listen to the kick drums. The Deluge's comes after the track's: hold ◀▶ pressed and turn TEMPO to the right. It comes before: to the left. Each single detent moves the Deluge about 10 ms.
  8. Mix: launch clips, set levels, and for transitions switch the filter section to DJ (chapter 8).

5. The Scan view: tempo and key

Open it from Song view: SHIFT + press SELECT → Tuning → Scan: note, BPM, key (7-segment: TUNE, SCAN). The item is there only when you open Settings from Song view. Leave it with BACK, SONG or SCALE (lit while Scan is open): back to Song view. The song keeps playing meanwhile, but the pads can't launch clips in the Scan view.

The title names the input: SCAN LINE (a jack in the line input), SCAN MIC IN, SCAN MIC (the internal microphone), or the channel you chose. A master tune other than 440 Hz follows it, e.g. SCAN LINE 432. The 7-segment display shows the big value only: 128.0, AMIN, or ---- for nothing yet.

5.1 Tempo

  • Appears after about 4 to 5 s of music and is renewed every second: the middle of the last three estimates, each over the last 8 s. After a change of tempo it follows within several seconds.
  • Range 60 to 207 BPM, weighted towards 120. It needs a beat: at least 4 clear hits in 8 s. A beatless intro or breakdown shows -- BPM.
  • Half or double: the music doesn't always say which it is. Drum and bass at 174 often reads as 87, and a slow track can read double. Use ◀▶ to show what you hear.
  • In the tests, drum loops read within 0.1 BPM of their tempo.

5.2 Key

  • Appears after about 4 s of music with notes, follows the music over about 10 s, and goes away after 10 s without notes. Drums alone and a single held note give no key (--).
  • Major and minor only. On dense music expect some errors to neighbouring keys: the relative key (A minor for C major) or a fifth away.
  • Camelot: A minor is 8A, C major 8B. Keys that mix well: the same number, one up or down (8A with 7A or 9A), or the same number with the other letter (8A with 8B).

The pads show the tuner's needle (for tuning an instrument); they are not needed for DJing. Scan costs about 2 % of the CPU while it is open and nothing when it is closed.

6. Sync: the song at the track's tempo

In the Scan view, press TAP TEMPO. The song's tempo becomes the BPM shown, with ×2 or ÷2 applied.

Synced: the song is now at 128 BPM
No tempo heard yet: the tempo stays
  • 7-segment: SYNC, or ---- when there is no tempo yet.
  • While playing it works at once: the next ticks follow the new tempo, nothing restarts. So you can sync again at any time.
  • The song gets the tempo as measured, which can have more decimals than the one shown.
  • Undo: back in Song view, BACK undoes the sync, SHIFT + BACK redoes it. (In the Scan view BACK leaves the view.)
  • EXTERNAL CLOCK (7-segment EXT): the Deluge follows a MIDI or trigger clock, which sets the tempo. Sync changes nothing then.
Whole numbers. Most electronic tracks have a whole-number tempo. If Scan reads 127.9 and the track is at 128, set 128 exactly: in Song view turn TEMPO one detent right and one left. With the community setting Fine tempo knob on (the default) the tempo encoder moves in whole BPM, so this lands on the nearest whole number. (On a 7-segment Deluge the first detent only shows the tempo.) An error of 0.1 BPM at 128 moves the beats apart by about 23 ms in 30 s.

7. Nudge: the beats together

While the song plays, in any view, hold the horizontal encoder ◀▶ pressed and turn the TEMPO encoder. To the right the Deluge moves ahead (earlier), to the left it moves back (later). Each detent shows SYNC NUDGED (7-segment NUDGE). In Song view, pressing ◀▶ first shows the zoom level (e.g. 16TH-NOTES): that is normal.

How it works

  • Each detent makes the tempo 4 % faster (right) or slower (left) for 0.25 s after it, like a hand on a record. That moves the Deluge by about 10 ms at any tempo. Then the tempo is exactly what it was.
  • Keep turning: the bend stays at 4 %, it doesn't add up. It lasts until 0.25 s after the last detent. So what counts is how long you turn: about 40 ms per second of turning, plus 10 ms for the last detent (figure 3).
  • For small corrections: single detents with a short pause between them, 10 ms each.

  • Clock outputs follow: the MIDI clock and the trigger clock output are bent with the Deluge, so gear synced to it moves along.
  • Stopping (PLAY) ends a bend at once. A song saved during a bend saves the tempo without it. If you set the tempo during a bend (tempo encoder, sync), the new tempo stays.
  • When stopped, the nudge does nothing: there is no beat to move.
  • Far off? At 128 BPM a beat lasts 469 ms. For more than about 100 ms, it is quicker to stop and press PLAY again on the next one.
Which way? Listen to the kick drums. Two kicks close together sound like one smeared kick (a "flam"): the beats are 10 to 30 ms apart. If the Deluge's kick is the second of the two, turn right; if it is the first, turn left. When the flam is gone, stop. Check again after a minute.

8. The DJ filter

Switch it on: in Song view (AFFECT ENTIRE lit, no clip pad held) the gold knobs work on the whole song. Select the filter section with its mod button (Cutoff/Resonance, lit). 8.1 What it moves

The DJ knob has no filter of its own: it moves the song's own LPF and HPF cutoff, exactly as the gold knobs in LPF and HPF would. That is why it sounds like the v18 filters (no stepping, no zipper), and why saving, undo and automation recording work as for those knobs. The song saves its filter type as dj; an older firmware loads LPF there, with the same cutoffs.

  • Set apart before: if both filters were on (a band-pass set in LPF and HPF, or by automation), the DJ knob goes on from there without a jump. To the right the low-pass opens first until it is off, then the high-pass opens. To the left the high-pass closes first, then the low-pass.
  • Automation recorded with the DJ knob is automation of the LPF and HPF cutoffs. To delete it, switch to LPF, hold SHIFT and press the upper knob: AUTOMATION DELETED; the same in HPF for the high-pass. Then press on to DJ.
  • What it filters: the whole song after its delay and reverb, so their tails are filtered too, and in setup A the player as well. The frequency drone (v12) joins after the song's effects and is not filtered.
Resonance. A little resonance (about 10 to 25 of 50) gives the typical peak of a DJ filter. High values can make the filters sing by themselves, the 24 dB ladder most of all; the firmware doesn't limit it. Start low and raise it while listening.

8.2 Classic moves

  • Build-up: turn slowly right over 8 or 16 bars: the bass goes away, the mix gets thin and tense. On the drop, back to the middle in one quick turn.
  • Take out, bring in: close the low-pass slowly (left) to make a part distant and dark, then fade it; bring the next one in from dark to open.
  • Hide the bass during a transition: with a mixer (setup B), the Deluge's channel a little to the right (high-pass) while the player's bass plays, then swap on a phrase boundary.

9. Mixing with it

9.1 Playing along with a track (setup A)

  1. Prepare a song: your parts in clips (drums, bass, pads), the audio track for the input (chapter 3.1), the filter section on DJ and in the middle. Save it.
  2. Start the track on the player. Scan, then TAP TEMPO (chapters 5, 6). Back to Song view.
  3. Start the Deluge on the one with a quiet part only (hi-hats, a single clip): mute the rest. Nudge until the kicks or hats land together.
  4. Bring your parts in by launching or unmuting clips at the start of a phrase (usually every 8 or 16 bars).
  5. Transitions: the DJ filter on everything, the player's own volume (or the audio track's) for the track alone.
  6. Check the phase every minute and nudge when it drifts (chapter 9.3).
Countdowns stay on top (v19.0.2). When you load the next song while one plays, or arm clips or a section to launch, the countdown (BARS REMAINING 3) stays on the title line and nothing covers it: the DJ filter's popup, a knob's value or the filter type appear below it. So you can take the old song out with the DJ filter and still see when the change comes. In Song view, keep AFFECT ENTIRE lit: with it off, the gold knobs there work on nothing.

9.2 With a DJ mixer (setup B)

  1. The player on channel 1, the Deluge on channel 2 with its fader down. Sync in Scan as above.
  2. Start the Deluge on the one and pre-listen on the mixer's cue. Nudge until it sits.
  3. Bring it in with the channel fader; the Deluge's DJ filter now works on its channel only, like a channel filter.
  4. To go on from the Deluge to the next track, beatmatch the player to the Deluge on the player as usual (its pitch fader), or scan the next track and sync the Deluge to it.

9.3 Staying in time

  • Sync gets the tempo to within about 0.1 BPM; that still drifts about 23 ms in 30 s at 128 BPM. Round to the whole number when the track has one (chapter 6), and nudge now and then.
  • If the player's tempo changes (a pitch fader moved, a tempo change in the track), sync again: in the Scan view while playing, then nudge.
  • A vinyl turntable is never quite steady: expect to nudge more often.

9.4 In key

The key page of the Scan view shows the track's key and Camelot number. Play your melodic parts in that key or a neighbour on the wheel (chapter 5.2): set your clips' scale and root note to it as usual on the Deluge. A one-press "song key from the key heard" is planned for v19.2.

12. Limits and what comes next

  • No cue on the Deluge itself: the headphones carry the main mix, so pre-listening needs a DJ mixer (setup B).
  • The phase is by ear: sync sets the tempo, not where the beat falls. There is no automatic beat alignment yet.
  • Tempo reading needs a beat, 60 to 207 BPM (beyond that with ×2 and ÷2), and about 4 s.
  • Key reading: major and minor only; some errors to neighbouring keys on dense music.
  • One DJ filter for the whole song mix, not per deck. For one track alone, use that track's own filter.
  • Sync and the new nudge need the Deluge's own clock. With an external clock the nudge works as in 1.2.1.
  • Not yet tested on a real Deluge (see the status box on page 1).

Planned (research/DJ-TOOL-PLAN.md): v19.1 the Deluge's own tracks as decks: a computer tool writes each track's tempo into its file, and audio clips then play whole tracks in sync, bar-exact. v19.2 a crossfader and an isolator EQ (kills for bass, mids and treble) on the Deluge's own controls, and the song's key set from the key heard.
