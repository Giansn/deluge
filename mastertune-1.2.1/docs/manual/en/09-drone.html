9. The frequency drone

The drone plays up to 16 sustained tones under the music of your song. Each tone can be steady or carry a beat: binaural, monaural or isochronic, free in hertz or synced to the tempo, also in triplets. You set the pitch in hertz or as a note that follows the master tune. Isochronic pulses have attack and release. The drone ducks under the song's sidechain, can send to the song reverb and is saved with the song. With drone tracks, its tones also live in kits, so clips and the arranger can switch and automate them.

Start quiet. Sustained tones, especially low ones and many at once, can seem louder than expected on headphones. Start at a low volume and raise it slowly.
Not a therapy: The names Delta to Gamma only describe frequency ranges. How beats affect people is scientifically disputed. The drone is a sound tool, not a medical device.

9.1 Quick start

  1. In song view, press the SCALE button. The drone view opens and the Scale LED lights up.
  2. In the bottom row, press the mute pad (first column to the right of the grid). It turns green and tone 1 sounds: a sine at 200 Hz.
  3. Turn the upper gold knob to change the pitch: 1 Hz per detent, 10 Hz when turned fast.
  4. Press the KIT button: the tone becomes binaural. On headphones you now hear a beating of 10 Hz.
  5. Turn the lower gold knob to change the beat, or press it so the beat follows the tempo (1/16 at first).
  6. Go back to song view with BACK, SONG or SCALE. The drone keeps sounding.

Alternatively, open the drone view in song view by pressing SELECT (song menu) → Drone. The arranger does not have this menu item, because the way back always leads to song view.

9.2 The drone view

The view is built like song view: one row per tone instead of one per clip, tone 1 at the bottom. Eight tones are visible at once. Use the Y encoder (vertical) to scroll to tones 9–16.

Pads in a row

  • Bar = level of the tone, 16 pads for 0–50.
  • Tapping a pad sets the level and selects the tone: pad 1 = 3, pad 8 = 25, pad 16 = 50.
  • Selected tone: its row glows faint gray behind the bar.
  • Tones that are off are strongly dimmed; their settings are kept.
  • Steady display: the pads light evenly and do not flash with the beat.

Colors and side columns

  • Orange: Tone (steady)
  • Blue: Binaural
  • Turquoise: Monaural
  • Violet: Isochronic
  • Mute column: green = on, dark red = off. Tapping toggles the tone and selects it.
  • Audition column: tapping selects the tone. White = selected, otherwise the mode color, dimmed.

9.4 Display: OLED and 7-segment

OLED

  • Title: number and mode of the selected tone, e.g. 3: Binaural. If the tone is off, - off follows.
  • Large: the pitch, 200.00 Hz or as a note, e.g. A2 +12c.
  • Bottom line:
    • Tone mode: timbre and level, e.g. Sine Level 40
    • other modes: beat with frequency band, e.g. Beat 10.00 Hz Alpha
    • with tempo sync: 1/16: 8.00 Hz Alpha, as a triplet 1/16T: 12.00 Hz Beta
  • Short pop-ups show level, pan, mode, Hz/Note, SYNC/Beat, the selected knob section and its values, e.g. Attack 12%.

7-segment

  • Frequency with as many decimal places as fit: 55.25, 440.0, 1200.
  • Note as a name, e.g. A2 (sharp shown as a dot).
  • While turning, shown briefly:
    • Level: number
    • Pan: number
    • Beat: in tenths, e.g. 7.8
    • Note value: 16 for 1/16, 16T for the triplet

Note names follow the Deluge numbering: A3 = 440 Hz, middle C = C3.

9.5 The four modes

ModeWhat you hearRemark
ToneA steady sustained tone, the same left and right (mind the pan).Beat and sync play no role.
BinauralLeft the frequency minus half the beat, right plus half the beat. The beating only arises in the head.Works only with headphones; over speakers it sounds like Monaural.
MonauralThe same two tones, but both in both ears. The beating is audible in the sound itself, also over speakers.Good for rooms and recordings.
IsochronicOne tone that switches on and off with the beat. Attack and release set how softly it starts and stops.Clearest effect, also over speakers.

Attack and release of the isochronic pulse

The pulse is on for half of each beat cycle. Attack is the time in which it rises at the start, Release the time in which it then fades. Both go from 0 to 50. Each step is 2 % of half the cycle length, so 50 is the whole half that is on. This way the pulse keeps its shape when the beat or tempo changes. An edge is never shorter than 1 ms, so nothing clicks. Both start at 6: at 10 Hz that is 6 ms, as in v12.

Attack / ReleaseSound
0–6clear, hard pulse
15–25soft, without edges
50 / 50almost sinusoidal swelling and fading
50 / 0swells and cuts off, like a tone played backwards

A new mode or a new timbre is faded briefly through silence, so nothing clicks. Level, pitch, beat and pan glide smoothly to the new value.

9.6 Pitch: hertz or note

In hertz

10.00 to 5000.00 Hz, accurate to 0.01 Hz. Useful for fixed frequencies, for example 432 Hz, 528 Hz or a room resonance. A frequency in hertz stays fixed, even if you change the Master Tune.

As a note

Note 0–127 plus −50 to +50 cents. The tone follows the Master Tune (Settings → Tuning) like all other sounds of the Deluge. If you tune the Master Tune to an external instrument, the drone matches automatically.

If you switch between Hz and note with a knob press or in the menu (Pitch as), the pitch is kept. A frequency becomes the nearest note with cents; a note becomes its frequency at the current Master Tune.

9.7 Beat and tempo sync

The beat is the beating or pulse frequency of the Binaural, Monaural and Isochronic modes. Free, it runs from 0 to 100 Hz in 0.01 Hz steps; it starts at 10 Hz. With tempo sync it follows the song tempo, straight or as a triplet (three in the time of two, so 1.5 times as fast):

9.8 Timbres

TimbreSound
Sinepure sine, clearest for beats

Pulse (v15)square wave with adjustable width (Pulse width 5–50 %, default 30): hollow and nasal when narrower, full at 50 %. With Life the width drifts slowly.

All timbres are band-limited: harmonics above 18 kHz are dropped, so nothing aliases or chirps on high tones. You choose the timbre in the menu (Timbre). Pulse applies to the single tone, the width to the whole drone.

9.8a Life and FM (v15)

A sustained tone soon sounds rigid. With Life, each tone moves by itself, as if played by hand; with FM it gets a color that blooms and fades again. Both apply to the whole drone, but each tone moves on its own paths.

SettingEffect
Life 0–50Each tone drifts on slow random paths that never repeat. At 50: pitch ±6 cents (path at about 0.08 Hz), volume ±3 dB ("breath"), brightness and stereo position ("shimmer"). Everything grows evenly with Life. On a beating tone, both sides drift together: the beat stays exactly as set.
Rate 0–50How fast Life and the blooming of the FM move. 25 as designed; every 12.5 steps doubles or halves the speed.
FM 0–50A modulator at the tone's frequency plus 0.3 Hz colors it. The depth blooms and fades, each tone on its own path. On high tones it is limited, so nothing audibly aliases.
FM formSine: soft, bell-like · Saw: brighter, more tinny. Switching crossfades.
  • At Life 0 and FM 0 (and without Pulse) the drone sounds exactly as in v13 and v14.
  • Changes to Life, Rate and FM glide; nothing clicks. A switch to or from Pulse goes through silence, like any change of timbre.
  • Life only moves what the drone itself does. Tempo sync, sidechain and Master Tune still apply as set.

9.9 Level, pan, volume and mix

  • Level per tone, 0–50 in 1 dB steps, 0 is silent. A tone at level 50 with drone volume 50 sits at −12 dBFS. New tones start at level 40.
  • Pan per tone, −32 to +32, equal power: 0 dB in the center, up to +3 dB on one side at the far ends. With Binaural, pan acts as a balance between the ears: panned hard, one ear barely hears its tone and the beating is lost.
  • Volume (in the menu): volume of the whole drone, 0–50 in 1 dB steps, 50 = 0 dB, starts at 40.
  • Song volume: the drone follows it, like the metronome.
  • In the mix the drone is added after the song effects, so without delay and song filter. It can be heard over USB audio (v8).
  • Reverb (menu Reverb amount or lower gold knob in the Sidechain/Reverb section): send of the whole drone into the song's reverb, 0–50. At 50 it sends as much as a sound with the reverb knob turned fully up. The send follows level, pan and sidechain ducking and glides smoothly when turned.
Many loud tones together can clip: 16 tones at level 50 add up to far more than full scale. For pad sounds of many tones, lower the level per tone or the drone volume.

9.12 Saving, loading, export

  • Saving: the drone is saved with the song (tag <drone>), but only if it has been set up. Older firmware simply skips it.
  • Loading: when you load another song or use Clear Song, the drone fades out in about 60 ms. The new song's drone starts from silence.
  • Stem export: the drone is not in the stems. Afterwards it starts again from silence.
  • Recording: you record it with the rest of the song via USB audio or the output.

9.13 With external devices

Set the tones to Note and tune the Master Tune (Settings → Tuning) to your device, for example a volca keys. Then the drone and the instrument share the same tuning. If you move the Master Tune later, all tones set as notes follow; tones in Hz stay fixed.

9.16 Drone tracks (v16)

The song drone sounds as long as it is on. With drone tracks you place drones where you want them: a drone track is a kit whose rows are drone tones. You launch their clips in song view and place them in the arranger like any kit clip. Several drone tracks can overlap, each with its kit's effects, volume and sidechain.

This creates a new kit (DRONE1, DRONE2 …) with 16 rows, the tones of the song drone; the tones that are on get a note across the whole clip. The clip sits at the bottom of song view and is not launched yet. The song drone itself stays unchanged.

Recording and playing Hz changes

  • PLAY, then RECORD, then turn Select or the upper gold knob: each change is written as a node into the row's Hz track and plays back in the next loop. Between two nodes the tone glides in about 15 ms. This also works when recording into the arranger.
  • Without RECORD, the row's base pitch changes; a recorded Hz track shifts with it.
  • While RECORD is on: SHIFT + Select records the fine 0.1 Hz steps. SHIFT + upper gold knob deletes the Hz track, like any automation; the row goes back to its base pitch at once.
  • The Hz track belongs to the row, not to the notes: recording notes, moving them or spreading them in a Euclidean pattern leaves it unchanged.
  • MIDI pitch bend on a drone row acts within the row's bend range (default 2 semitones) and is also recorded while RECORD is on.

Good to know

  • CPU time: about 0.3–0.45 % CPU per sounding row, 16 rows 5–7 %. Rows with a closed gate cost nothing.
  • A new drone kit sounds as loud as the song drone at the same setting.
  • Older firmware opens songs with drone tracks without the drone rows; the other rows stay correct. If you save them there, the drone rows are gone.
