3. Lighter load and the CPU monitor

mastertune does the same work with fewer instructions and wastes less time between audio blocks. The CPU monitor shows you the load, the voices, and when the Deluge has to save on quality or cut voices.

3.1 Measured against 1.2.1

Stock 1.2.1 and v18.3 rendered the same songs in the emulator, both at 440 Hz. The first measure counts the instructions the Deluge needs per window of 128 samples (2.9 ms). That is its DSP work.

The second measure runs the firmware's own task manager and records how much of the time goes into the audio routine:

IPC is the number of instructions per clock cycle. The value on the device is unknown. Lower values stand in, roughly, for cache misses and memory waits. At IPC 0.7 the Sitar song lowers its quality in 32% of the renders with v18.3 and in all of them with 1.2.1.

What it means:

  • About 20–25% less DSP work per sample, the same in all three songs.
  • At moderate load the audio routine's time can roughly halve (the Sitar song: −44 to −54%). 1.2.1 computed in tiny blocks with a fixed overhead per track (3.2). Near full load this advantage disappears: both then use nearly all the time and differ only in how often they lower the quality and cut voices.
  • More voices before cutting: 32 instead of 20.4 on average in the full-load song. Part of it comes from the lighter DSP, part from the gentler culling taken over from the current community firmware.
  • "Half the load" is not right as a general claim. It holds only at moderate load.
Caveats. The emulator has no memory or cache model, so the L2 cache is not in these numbers; on the device it should add to the gain. The audio of v18.3 is not bit-identical to 1.2.1, because v18 changed filters and saturation (chapter 5); voices and level match. Streaming songs, 432 Hz and UI or MIDI load were not part of this comparison.

3.3 Sample streaming and the Kit RAM saver v9

The SD card does not get faster, but its throughput now goes where it is needed:

  • Loading by urgency. 1.2.1 sorted its load jobs by memory address instead of priority. Now what a playing voice needs next comes first. You get fewer voices breaking off and fewer "card too slow" messages under load, for example when you change kits while playing.
  • Double reserve. A playing voice keeps three clusters ahead instead of two: with 32 KB clusters about 250–370 ms instead of 120–190 ms (stereo). It costs 32 KB of RAM per voice that is streaming.
  • Short samples stay in RAM. Samples of up to four clusters (128 KB with 32 KB clusters) now really stay in memory, as intended in 1.2.1.
  • No lost hits. If a sample's start is not in RAM when you strike it, the voice waits until it is loaded (usually a few milliseconds, at most 100 ms) and plays from the beginning. 1.2.1 dropped the hit.

Kit RAM saver: Settings → Community features → Kit RAM saver (7-segment KRAM), on by default. Kit rows without notes in all of the song's clips release the held start of their samples, usually 64 KB per sample. A kit with 50 samples of which the song uses 5 frees around 3 MB. The samples stay in the kit. When a row gets notes, the Deluge fetches its start back within one second. Not affected: kits with the kit arpeggiator or with MIDI learn for the whole kit, and rows with their own MIDI learn, because they can play without notes.

If you audition a row that has no notes, or play it over MIDI, its very first hit can come a few milliseconds late. Sequenced notes are not affected. If this bothers you, switch the Kit RAM saver off.

3.4 The CPU monitor

The CPU monitor puts one short line at the top left of the OLED, over whatever view you are in.

  • Menu: Settings → CPU monitor (7-segment CPU): Off, On, Alerts or Profile.
  • Shortcut: hold LEARN and press the TEMPO knob. v17
ModeWhat you see
OffNothing. Switched off, the monitor costs a single check per pass of the audio routine.

ProfileThe line as with On. In addition the Deluge sends measurements for the profiler to the computer (8.6). v16
  • The shortcut switches between Off and the mode you used last (On or Alerts), in every view and also while playing. It confirms briefly, on the 7-segment display C-ON, COFF or C-AL.
  • Saved at once: the mode goes to the card immediately (CommunityFeatures.XML) and applies again after a restart. Profile is chosen in the menu; after a restart it counts as On. The startup song is left untouched.
  • The real load: from v17 on, passes that compute nothing don't count as busy. Up to v16 an idle Deluge showed 85–90%. That was busy waiting, not overload.
  • To the computer: with the monitor on, the Deluge sends one message per second on USB MIDI port 3 only. DIN MIDI never gets anything. If the USB send buffer is full, the message is skipped, so notes and clock keep priority. Chapter 8 shows how to log it.

3.5 When QL or VC appear

QL means the audio routine is behind the output. The Deluge then saves on quality: simpler oscillator tables, linear instead of sinc interpolation. From about 80 samples behind it switches voices off: that is VC. What costs CPU:
