Drone with volca keys

Manual for the Deluge with firmware 1.2.1-mastertune v16 (or newer) and a Korg volca keys

The volca keys is a small analog synthesizer with three oscillators, one filter and one envelope. Played from the Deluge, it delivers long, warm tones that move slowly. The Deluge's Frequency Drone lays exact tones and beatings underneath: binaural, monaural or isochronic. This manual shows you how to wire, tune, play and record both.

1. What the combination is good for

What the volca keys adds

  • Analog sound that drifts slightly and so feels alive
  • Three oscillators: as a chord of up to three notes (Poly) or as one wide tone (Unison, Octave, Fifth)
  • Filter, LFO and delay for slow movement
  • Remote control via MIDI: notes, pitch bend and 16 control changes (CC)

What the Deluge adds

  • Long notes in a MIDI clip, CC automation, MIDI clock
  • The Frequency Drone with exact tones: Tone, Binaural, Monaural, Isochronic, with sidechain
  • Drone tracks (v16) that you launch like clips
  • Recording the volca via the line in, also as an audio clip that follows the Master Tune

Typical uses: an analog pad over a binaural foundation, a filter sweep over many bars, a slow beating between the drifting analog tone and the exact drone tone, or a recording of the volca that you then retune to 432 Hz.

Rule of thumb: The volca keys is fixed at A = 440 Hz. Leave the Master Tune at 440.0 and set the drone tones to Note. Then the volca and the drone match. For 432 Hz and other tunings, see chapter 5.

3. Setting up the volca keys

According to the Korg manual, you reach the volca's global settings by switching it on while holding the FUNC button. The parameters are then on the step buttons: 1 Auto Power Off, 2 battery type, 3 SYNC OUT polarity, 4 SYNC IN polarity, 6 MIDI clock, 7 MIDI ShortMessage. REC saves and restarts, PLAY cancels without saving.

SettingFor this setupRemark
MIDI channelleave at 1, the defaultThe Deluge clip then also goes to channel 1. The sources differ on exactly how to change the channel: buttons 1–16 directly, via MEMORY or with FUNC + button 16 verify
MIDI clockLED of step 6 on = Auto (follows MIDI clock), off = internalOnly matters if the volca's sequencer or Motion Sequence should run at the Deluge's tempo. Under MIDI clock, the volca's tempo knob has no effect.
SYNC IN polarityrising edge (default)global parameter on button 4; only matters with a sync cable
Auto Power Offoff for long dronesButton 1. Otherwise the volca switches itself off after about 4 hours.
MIDI ShortMessageleave at defaultButton 7. What exactly the parameter does is not documented verify. If the volca does not respond to notes or CCs from the Deluge, switch it.

If a cable is plugged into SYNC IN, the volca's sequencer follows only the pulses and ignores its own clock. The Korg manual also lists the tempo range as a global parameter. It plays no role in this setup, because the tempo comes from the Deluge.

4. Setting up the Deluge

MIDI clip for the volca

Setting up a MIDI clip is part of the standard operation of 1.2.1. The mastertune documents do not describe it; the official Deluge manual explains it in detail. In short:

  1. Create a new clip as usual and switch to clip view.
  2. Press the MIDI button. The clip now plays to a MIDI channel, and the MIDI LED lights up.
  3. Turn Select until the volca's channel appears (default: 1).
  4. Enter notes. For drones: long notes, see chapter 6.

5. Tuning: volca and Master Tune

The volca keys has neither a Master Tune nor a fine tuning. So A = 440 Hz is effectively fixed. The Deluge's Master Tune therefore shifts everything in the Deluge, but not the volca.

Sound sourceFollows the Master Tune?
Drone tones as Noteyes
Drone tones in Hzno, the frequency stays fixed
Deluge synths, kits, samplesyes
Audio clips, including recordings of the volcayes, by time-stretch; the tempo stays in sync. A recording is only shifted by the difference from the tuning it was recorded at, never twice.
volca, played via MIDIWith a tuning other than 440 Hz, the Deluge sends RPN 1 (Channel Fine Tuning) on its channel. That the volca evaluates RPN 1 is not documented and, given the missing fine tuning, unlikely verify. Expect it to stay at 440.
volca via line in as an oscillatorno, it sounds unchanged in its own tuning

The volca's auto-tuning

The volca retunes itself: after about 10 seconds of silence it corrects the drift of its oscillators. This runs continuously; no warm-up time is needed. For drones this means verify: as long as a sustained tone sounds, it does not retune and can drift slightly over time. Give it more than 10 seconds of silence between two pieces, for example by stopping with PLAY on the Deluge.

At 440 Hz (recommended)

Master Tune at 440.0, and all drone tones that should match the volca set to Note (press the upper gold knob or use Pitch as in the menu). This is the approach from chapter 13 of the Drone manual: tune the Master Tune to the external device. For the volca that means 440.0. If the Master Tune has never been set to anything else since power-on, the Deluge also sends no RPN 1.

At 432 Hz: honest options

From 440 to 432 Hz is −31.77 cents. The volca cannot do this by itself. There are three ways:

WayHow toDrawback
A. Record, then retune (safest way)Master Tune at 440.0, record the volca as an audio clip (chapter 8). Then Master Tune to 432.0: the clip sounds 31.77 cents lower, the tempo stays. Drone tones set as notes follow.Not live. Time-stretch needs more CPU time and has very quiet artifacts. Important: A recording made at Master Tune 432.0 counts as 432 material and would stay at the volca's pitch, that is 440.
B. Pitch bendLower the volca by about 32 cents with pitch bend. With a bend range of ±2 semitones, that is about 16 % of the way down (14-bit value about 6890 instead of 8192). Check with a tuner.The bend range comes from a forum. Resolution and interaction with the auto-tuning are not documented verify. How the Deluge holds a fixed bend value in a MIDI clip is not in the mastertune documents (official manual). Any further bend adds on top.
C. Deliberately against each otherMaster Tune 432.0, the volca stays at 440. The same notes then beat audibly against each other: at A2 by 4 Hz, at A3 by 8 Hz.Only useful as an effect, not as a tuning.
A drone tone at 432 Hz in Hz is not the same as Master Tune 432.0: it stays fixed, even if you change the Master Tune. At Master Tune 432.0 the note A3 gives exactly 432 Hz.

6. Playing drones on the volca

Long notes

  • Enter long notes in the MIDI clip, for a pad across the whole clip. The volca holds the tone as long as the note lasts.
  • A new note restarts the envelope for all held notes (secondary source). So for a calm pad, start all notes of a chord at the same time. Staggered entries create a new attack each time.
  • A note that starts at step 1 and fills the whole clip is held by the Deluge across the loop boundary. It sends neither Note Off nor Note On there, so the volca does not retrigger.
  • In Poly mode the volca plays up to three notes. What happens with more than three notes is not documented verify. Stick to three at most.

Voice modes (VOICE)

ModeSoundFor drones
Poly3 notes, sawtoothchord of up to three notes, for example root, fifth, octave
Unisonmono, all three oscillators on one notewide, carrying single tone; beating with Detune
Octavemonosingle tone with an octave sound

Poly Ringsquare wave with ring modulationmetallic chord

Via MIDI, CC 40 selects the mode. Which values give which mode is not documented verify. So set the mode with the volca's knob. The same applies to the octave setting (CC 41, 6 octaves on the knob).

Envelope for sustained tones

  • Attack high: the tone fades in slowly.
  • Sustain fully up: the tone holds its volume. That it then holds indefinitely is inferred verify.
  • Decay/Release long: the tone fades out softly.
  • There is only one envelope and one filter (12 dB) for all three voices.

Detune and portamento

  • Detune (CC 42) detunes the oscillators against each other. In Unison this creates an inner beating that makes the tone wide.
  • Portamento (CC 5) makes the tone glide from note to note. With long notes this becomes a slow drift.

Slow filter sweeps

  • Via CC: automate CC 44 (Cutoff) in the MIDI clip over many bars. How to record or draw CCs in a MIDI clip is part of the standard operation of 1.2.1 (official manual). The volca has no MIDI OUT, so the route always goes through the Deluge.
  • Via LFO: LFO Rate (CC 46) low, LFO Cutoff Int (CC 48) to taste. Waveforms sawtooth, triangle, square. The LFO can be synced to the start of the note (Trigger Sync).
  • Steps: according to a review, the filter steps audibly at high resonance. For very slow sweeps, keep the resonance moderate.
  • Delay: Delay Time (CC 52) and Feedback (CC 53) add space. Feedback also sets the amount of delay in the sound.

7. Together with the Frequency Drone

You open the song drone in song view with SCALE or via SELECT → song menu → Drone. The Drone manual describes how to operate it. This chapter is about how the two play together.

Hitting the same notes

  • Set the drone tone to Note: press the upper gold knob or use Pitch as in the menu. Turning the upper gold knob selects the note, with SHIFT the cents.
  • The note names follow the Deluge numbering: A3 = 440 Hz, middle C = C3. Because the Deluge sends the volca its notes, this numbering applies equally to the MIDI clip and the drone.
  • For a foundation, place the drone one or two octaves below the volca's root note. For warmth, a fifth or octave above works well.

Binaural beat under an analog chord

The volca plays a chord in Poly mode. Below it are one or two drone tones in Binaural mode (KIT button) on the root note, one or two octaves lower. The beating arises only in the drone; the volca provides the pad above it. This works only with headphones.

Beating between analog and digital

If you put a drone tone on the same note as the volca and detune it by a few cents, the two tones slowly beat against each other. The beating is about frequency × cents ÷ 1731: at A2 (220 Hz) and 3 cents, about 0.4 Hz. Because the volca drifts slightly, the beating drifts with it. This sounds alive.

Sidechain

The drone can duck under the song's sidechain triggers, for example under a kick with Send to sidechain. In the drone menu under Sidechain you find Volume ducking, SYNC, ATTACK/RELEASE and SHAPE. The volca does not duck along; its pad stays steady while the drone pumps underneath.

Drone tracks (v16)

In the drone view, SHIFT + KIT creates a kit DRONE1 (DRONE2 …) from the drone tones. The rows' notes are the gate; a row's arpeggiator makes the tone pulse. You launch the clip in song view together with the volca's MIDI clip. That way the volca and the drone start and end together.

Level: a drone tone at Level 50 and Volume 50 sits at −12 dBFS. The volca comes in via the line in or the mixer. Balance the two by ear and start with a drone Level of about 30.

8. The volca through the Deluge

With a cable from the volca's headphone output into the Deluge's line in, you can record the volca or send it through the Deluge. Start with a low volume on the volca. The official Deluge manual describes in detail how to use audio clips and recording. The menu items are called Audio source, Input (left/right/stereo) and Monitoring.

  • 432 Hz: for way A from chapter 5, record at Master Tune 440.0 and only change it afterwards.

9. Recipes

Values on the volca are knob positions by ear. The Deluge values come from the Drone manual. All recipes assume MIDI channel 1 and Master Tune 440.0, except recipe 5.

1. Analog chord over a binaural base

  1. volca: VOICE to Poly, Attack about half, Sustain fully up, Decay/Release long, Cutoff half.
  2. Deluge: MIDI clip of 4 bars, with C3, E3 and G3 together across the whole clip.
  3. SCALE in song view. Tone 1: mute pad on, KIT (Binaural), Pitch as Note, note C2, Timbre Sine, Beat 6 Hz, Level 32.
  4. Tone 2: Binaural, note G2, Timbre Soft, Beat 6 Hz, Level 26.
  5. PLAY. Listen on headphones.

2. Unison drone with a filter sweep over 16 bars

  1. volca: VOICE to Unison, Detune about a quarter, Attack long, Sustain fully up, resonance moderate.
  2. Deluge: MIDI clip of 16 bars, one note A1 across the whole clip.
  3. Automate CC 44 (Cutoff) in the clip: from low to high over 8 bars, back over 8 bars.
  4. Drone tone 1: mode Tone (SYNTH), Pitch as Note (press the upper gold knob), note A0, Timbre Sine, Level 30. It carries the low end while the filter moves.

3. Slow beating against the volca

  1. volca: Unison, Detune fully down, Sustain fully up. MIDI clip with one long note A2.
  2. Drone tone 1: mode Tone, Timbre Soft, Pitch as Note (press the upper gold knob), note A2, then turn SHIFT + upper gold knob to +3 cents.
  3. Set Level so that the drone and the volca are about equally loud. This creates a beating of about 0.4 Hz.
  4. Optional: Life 10 (Delay section, upper gold knob) for extra movement.

4. Pulsing ring drone in tempo

  1. volca: VOICE to Poly Ring, Sustain fully up, Delay Time medium, Feedback low.
  2. Deluge: MIDI clip with two long notes a fifth apart, for example D2 and A2.
  3. Drone tone 1: CV (Isochronic), Pitch as Note (press the upper gold knob), note D1, Timbre Soft. Press the lower gold knob (SYNC) and turn it to 1/8T. Attack/Release section: Attack 20, Release 30.
  4. Add a kick with Send to sidechain, and set Volume ducking to 20 in the drone menu.

5. Bringing the volca to 432 Hz

  1. Settings → Tuning → Master tune (Hz) to 440.0.
  2. Set up the volca as in recipe 2 and record it via the line in into an audio clip, for example 8 bars.
  3. Master Tune to 432.0. The audio clip now sounds 31.77 cents lower; the tempo stays.
  4. Drone tones set as notes follow; A3 is now at 432 Hz. Tones in Hz stay fixed.
  5. Do not play the volca live along with it any more: it is still at 440.

12. Limits and open points

Limits

  • The volca keys has no fine tuning. The Deluge's Master Tune does not retune it; 432 Hz only works via recording or pitch bend (chapter 5).
  • Paraphonic: three voices share one filter and one envelope. There are no independent envelopes per note.
  • No MIDI OUT: the volca can send neither notes nor knob movements to the Deluge.
  • Auto-tuning only in pauses: with sustained tones lasting hours, the volca can drift slightly.

Open points verify

Not documented, or with conflicting sources: the MIDI IN socket as 5-pin DIN (very likely); the exact way to set the MIDI channel; the pulses per step at SYNC IN (probably 2 PPQN); the value ranges of CC 40 and CC 41, the effect of CC 11 and which parameters can only be reached via MIDI; pitch bend range, velocity, Program Change, RPN 1 and Local Control; the behavior with more than three notes and indefinite holding with Sustain fully up; whether a sustained tone suppresses the auto-tuning. On the Deluge side: a fixed pitch bend value in a MIDI clip.
